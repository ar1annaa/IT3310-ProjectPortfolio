## Agile Delivery Fit

I think that the patient scheduling workflow should be on an iterative delivery. This part of the delivery isn't just one ticket, its an entire section of the project that will need sub-tasks, testing, and careful review. By releasing this in iterative cycles, the team can learn directly from clinic staff and stakeholders to adapt features based on its real-world usage. However, areas such as project compliance, regulatory data security standards, and foundational system architecture will still require stronger advance planning and formal controls to ensure security and legal alignment from day one.

## Sprint Planning Reflection

The selected items form a cohesive Sprint 1 plan by targeting the foundational user workflows that RCHN needs most urgently: standardized intake and appointment scheduling. By combining the scheduling workflow prototype, intake form standardization, and the sprint communication update, the team reaches a total of 23 points, staying safely within the 25-point course planning limit while prioritizing high-value, high-risk workflow elements. This structure allows the team to learn what clinic staff and stakeholders expect from the core scheduling interface during the first review cycle. One high-priority item intentionally deferred is the clinic role permissions matrix; while critical for security, it should wait until the core intake fields and prototype are validated so that role-based access rules can be mapped directly to finalized feature components.

## Scrum Master Reflection

### Obstacle and Response
One common impediment that could affect progress toward the Sprint Goal is developers spending excessive time or getting stuck on specific ticket complexities without communicating blockers early. As the Scrum Master, I would help the team address this by facilitating daily stand-ups to surface bottlenecks promptly, coaching developers on breaking down large tasks into smaller increments, and helping them identify when to escalate. Rather than taking over the technical work or assigning solutions, I would empower the team to self-manage by guiding them to re-evaluate their daily sprint plan and collaborate on unblocking themselves.

### Preparing for Week 7
The Sprint Planning work completed this week helps establish a clear framework for building and prioritizing the Product Backlog next week by grounding feature discussions in actual capacity, priority levels, and user value. Having defined a concrete Sprint Goal and selected items based on urgency and risk, I now have a practical understanding of how to evaluate incoming requirements, order backlog items effectively, and write clear acceptance criteria that align technical delivery with stakeholder expectations.
